# GLOBAL-AEROPLANE-CRASHES-ROOT-CAUSE-ANALYSIS
Aviation accidents are complex events with mechanical, environmental, human, and systemic implications.

**Introduction**

Air travel is considered to be the safest way to travel, but aviation accidents still occur and are complex events with mechanical, environmental, human, and systemic implications. Each accident generates lessons that can inform design, maintenance, training, operational policy, and regulatory oversight. A structured root cause analysis helps convert complex accident records into practical safety intelligence.

As part of the Vephla University Simulations Capstone Programme, our team set out to analyse a dataset that follows a historical record of global aeroplane crashes from 1918 to 2022. The dataset has approximately 28,532 crash records that capture the following details: Flight conditions (date, time, aircraft type, operator, phase of flight), crash outcome (survival status, fatalities, causes) and contextual outcomes (technical, human, environmental causes).

The project involved 10 team members: 9 data analysts and 1 project manager.

Microsoft Excel was used for data extraction and cleaning.

Power BI was used for data modelling, data visualisation, and uncovering data insights.

**Key Insights Generated**

Total accidents — 28,457

Total fatalities — 159,000

Total survivors — 147,000

Total accidents with no casualties — 11,811

Total aircraft — 1,120

The North America region had the highest number of accidents (8179)

Human factors were the main cause of global crashes, at 34.79%

**Recommendations**

1. Strengthen Human Factor Risk Management: Since human factors are the leading cause of accidents, aviation stakeholders should invest in advanced pilot training programmes (simulations, emergency scenarios) and implement fatigue management systems.

2. Improve Aircraft Maintenance & Monitoring Systems, with technical failures ranking high; organisations should adopt predictive maintenance using data analytics, increase the frequency of safety inspections and integrate real-time aircraft health monitoring systems.

3. Focus on High-Risk Flight Phases: Since most accidents occur during these phases, enhance landing and take-off safety protocols, upgrade runway infrastructure and navigation systems and provide phase-specific pilot training.

4. Improve Data Collection & Investigation Processes: The high number of unknown causes indicates data gaps: Standardise global accident reporting systems, use data analytics and forensic tools for investigations, and encourage transparent reporting across aviation bodies.

**Limitations**

Although the dataset was massive and had a lot of variables to work with, it contained a lot of missing values (N/A, unknown) in columns such as YOM and MSM, which led to a limited analysis of regulatory changes over time.
